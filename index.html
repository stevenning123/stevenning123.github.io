Research

peer reviewed, first author, NSF funded

Feature engineering for network intrusion detection

June 2023 to July 2024

Cyberattack detectors get fed messy network data full of features that carry no signal. I tested how much the choice of feature selection method really matters, across both classical machine learning and deep learning, on 197,222 records from the NSL KDD dataset.

Random Forest reached 99.7% accuracy with entropy based selection. The more useful finding was the asymmetry: once the models had 20 features to work with, the deep learning results barely moved no matter which selection method produced them, varying by under one percent, while the classical models stayed sensitive to that choice. Deep models pull out what they need on their own, so the careful manual work pays off mainly on the classical side. I also configured and tuned the RNN, 50 LSTM units on an adam optimizer, to pick up temporal patterns in traffic.

I did this work while still at Saratoga High School, through the Silicon Valley Cybersecurity Institute, with researchers from San Jose State University. It made me first author on a peer reviewed IEEE paper before I started college. The research was supported by National Science Foundation Grant 2244597.

JaySchedule

April 2026 to June 2026

A weekly planner that reads your real calendar and inbox, then drafts the replies.

Built as a team. It pulls live Google Calendar and Gmail data, surfaces a prioritized task list, and uses Claude to draft every email reply in three tones, refreshed automatically each morning at 5:30 AM Pacific. Gemini generates the visual themes. It runs on serverless Netlify Functions with Google OAuth, shipped as a single file web app, then wrapped as a native iOS app with Capacitor. The hard part was never the model call. It was making something dependable enough to open every morning.
